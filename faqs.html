Frequently asked questions

1. What is The TASO APP Explorer?

The TASO APP Explorer tool is a searchable database that brings together targets, planned interventions, and provider information from English higher education providers’ access and participation plans (APPs). It allows you to filter, compare, map, and download APP data across the sector.

2. Who is the tool for, and how should it be used?

It is designed for higher education practitioners, researchers, and evaluators looking to collaborate and share learning. You can use it to find other institutions working on similar equality gaps, see what activities they plan to deliver, and explore regional patterns.

3. Can I use the APP Explorer to rank providers or judge their performance?

No, the tool is not a league table and should not be used to score or judge providers. The number of targets or activities listed reflects how a provider wrote its plan, not the quality, scale, or impact of its work.

4. Which providers and plans are included?

The dataset covers 267 providers registered with the Office for Students (OfS) in the approved (fee cap) category. It reflects the most recent plans available on the OfS website as of 10 August 2026, alongside a small number of older plans (marked as past plans) so that every published target and intervention remains searchable.

5. What information is included in the dataset?

The tool combines three key datasets:

  • Targets: the targets each provider has set, from the standard target tables in its plan.
  • Interventions: the activities each provider plans to deliver, from the activity tables in its intervention strategies.
  • Provider information: where each provider is based, its mission group and student population demographics, and a link to its plan.

6. Are all activities mentioned in an APP included?

No, the interventions dataset only captures activities listed in the structured activity tables of a plan’s intervention strategies. It excludes research and monitoring activities, as well as financial support or initiatives mentioned only in prose elsewhere in the document.

7. Why do records have both "standard labels" and the provider’s own wording?

Providers describe student characteristics and lifecycle stages in different ways. To let you filter across the whole sector, TASO used AI (Artificial Intelligence) to assign each record to standardised categories (such as ‘Area-based disadvantage’, ‘Care experienced and estranged’, or ‘Pre-entry attainment’). The provider's original wording is always preserved alongside these labels in the records and downloads.

8. What does "similar share of the same student group" mean when comparing providers?

When searching for providers with the same target, you can filter for institutions with a similar student demographic profile. "Similar" means the provider’s share of undergraduates in that student group is within 5 percentage points of your chosen provider.

9. How was AI used to build the APP Explorer?

AI was used to extract and categorise existing text from non-standardised APP PDFs so the data could be searched and filtered - a task that would otherwise take months of manual work.

10. How was the data checked for accuracy?

Every target and intervention went through a robust quality assurance process:

  • Targets: Because target tables follow a standard OfS format, a Python script checked every extracted field directly against the plan PDFs. Overall, 99.3% of fields matched exactly (or differed only in punctuation or standard labelling), and human reviewers manually checked and corrected every discrepancy (151 fields across 80 targets), as well as any tables published as images.
  • Interventions: Because activity tables vary widely in layout, records went through three layers of checking: a manual pilot review of five providers to set the rules; an automated field-by-field verification by a second AI model (checking 6,154 records and 67,694 fields against direct quotes and page numbers in the PDFs); and human review of all uncertain fields and disagreements, as well as a random sample of spot-checks.

11. Does that mean the data is 100% error-free?

No, whilst quality assurance significantly reduces errors, but both AI and human reviewers can make mistakes. For targets, we have >99% confidence in all details due to the standardised structure of how targets are captured in plans. For interventions, confidence levels vary across fields, with basic details like names and descriptions having >95% confidence, whereas fields such as the student groups that interventions are targeted towards having slightly lower confidence (75-80%). Every record links directly to the original APP PDF, and you should always verify details against the source document before relying on them.

12. Does the data show whether an intervention actually worked?

No, APPs set out what providers intend to do, not whether activities were delivered or how effective they were. For evidence on the impact of different widening participation and student success approaches, consult TASO’s Evidence Toolkit and Higher Education Evaluation Library.

13. Why isn't my institution, or a specific activity, showing up in a search?

There are three common reasons:

  1. The plan describes the work under a different heading or student group than the filter selected.
  2. The activity is part of the provider's wider practice outside its APP, or appears only in narrative text rather than an intervention strategy table.
  3. The plan was updated or varied after the dataset was collected.

14. How often is the APP Explorer updated?

Targets and interventions are updated annually when the OfS publishes new or revised plans. Formal variations made between annual updates may not appear immediately, so check the OfS website for the latest version of a provider's plan.

15. How do I report an error or share how I’ve used the tool?

You can use the feedback form on the APP Explorer page to flag any inaccuracies in your institution's data or to tell the team how you are using the resource.

16. How should I cite or credit The TASO APP Explorer in my work?

When referencing the tool or its dataset in reports, presentations, or research, you can use the following suggested citation:

Centre for Transforming Access and Student Outcomes in Higher Education (TASO). (2026). The TASO APP Explorer (Version 2) [Dataset/Online tool]. Available at: https://taso-he.github.io/APP_Explorer_External/ (Accessed: day month year).
